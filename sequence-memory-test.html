The Science of Visuospatial Sequence Recall

Inspired by classical neuropsychological tools like the Corsi Block-Tapping Test (developed by Philip Corsi in 1972) and cognitive primate benchmarks, the Sequence Memory Test evaluates your brain's capacity to encode, buffer, and reconstruct sequential spatial locations in short-term working memory.

How It Works:

The computer flashes a sequential pattern across the grid tiles. Once the sequence finishes, your goal is to tap the tiles in the exact same chronological order. Each successful round appends one additional step to the sequence, challenging the physiological limits of your spatial memory span.

Average Human Memory Span: Miller's Law (7 ± 2)

Cognitive psychologist George A. Miller famously demonstrated in 1956 that the standard human working memory capacity is approximately 7 ± 2 items (or chunks). Achieving Level 9 or higher indicates superior spatial chunking ability and strong hippocampal-parietal buffer integration.
