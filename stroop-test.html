The Science of the Stroop Effect & Cognitive Interference

Discovered in 1935 by psychologist John Ridley Stroop, the Stroop Effect is one of the most widely cited phenomena in experimental psychology. It demonstrates the conflict between two simultaneous mental processes: an automated reflex (reading a word) versus a controlled cognitive task (naming the physical color of the font).

How to Play:

In this 60-second test, color words will appear on your screen. The meaning of the word will often conflict with the font color (e.g., the word "BLUE" written in bright red ink). You must ignore what the word spells and immediately click the font color!

Neurobiology: The Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC)

Functional brain imaging proves that when humans experience Stroop conflict, two critical brain regions ignite:

  • Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC): Acts as the brain's executive conflict alarm. It detects discrepancies between automatic reading and deliberate color recognition.
  • Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex (DLPFC): Allocates top-down attention to suppress the wrong reaction and choose the correct color response.
